# Cross-Scripture Research Report v2

## Core separation rule
A revelation claim is not automatically identical to a modern canonical book. Identity must be independently demonstrated.

## Torah
The Qur'an explicitly describes the Torah as sent down to Moses (5:44). Current Jewish Torah organization uses five books. Ancient manuscript evidence includes the Dead Sea Scrolls, whose official digital library describes its collection as preserving the oldest known copies of biblical texts and making thousands of fragments accessible online.

## Zabur / Psalms
The Qur'an explicitly connects the Zabur with Dawud (4:163; 17:55). Psalms is a major textual witness in Jewish and Christian Bible traditions, but the project does not assume that the modern Psalms collection is identical in every respect to the Qur'anic Zabur.

## Injil / Gospels
The Qur'an explicitly says Allah gave Isa the Injil (5:46). Christian Bible structure contains four canonical Gospels: Matthew, Mark, Luke, and John. Their equivalence to the original Injil remains an identification question in this project.

## Christian manuscript witness
The Codex Sinaiticus Project dates Codex Sinaiticus generally to the fourth century and describes it as containing the whole New Testament and a substantial Septuagint Old Testament. The manuscript also contains corrections by early correctors, which is relevant to textual history.

## Conclusion
The defensible result at this stage is a separation between revelation claims, surviving textual witnesses, manuscript history, and later interpretation.
