# Divine Text Verification: Research Paper v2

## Abstract
This study proposes a source-critical framework for comparing Qur'anic revelation claims, hadith reports, Jewish and Christian textual traditions, manuscripts, and later interpretation.

## 1. Research Questions
1. What does each tradition explicitly claim is revelation?
2. What texts survive today as witnesses to those claims?
3. How early are surviving witnesses?
4. What textual variation is documented?
5. Where are links between a named revelation and a modern text strong, weak, or unresolved?

## 2. Method
The engine separates source level, textual transmission, historical evidence, and interpretation. Scores apply to claims, not entire religions.

## 3. Quran Baseline
The local project corpus contains 6,236 normalized ayah records derived from a local Uthmani witness. External manuscript evidence is kept as a separate evidence layer.

## 4. Hadith
Hadith remains a separate evidence layer. The project distinguishes isnad, narrator assessment, matn, parallel routes, classical grading, and historical assessment. Full remote corpus ingestion is currently pending.

## 5. Torah
The Qur'an explicitly identifies the Torah with Moses (5:44). Current Jewish Torah/Pentateuch structure is modeled as five books, while ancient manuscript witnesses are handled separately.

## 6. Zabur
The Qur'an explicitly identifies the Zabur with Dawud (4:163; 17:55). Psalms is treated as a candidate modern textual witness rather than an automatically identical document.

## 7. Injil
The Qur'an explicitly identifies the Injil with Isa (5:46). Matthew, Mark, Luke, and John are modeled as four canonical Gospels; their equivalence to the original Injil is an unresolved identification claim.

## 8. Bible Canon
The engine supports multiple Christian canon profiles rather than assuming one universal table of contents.

## 9. Limitations
Missing primary corpora reduce some scores to PENDING. Manuscript dating does not establish divine origin.

## 10. Conclusion
The framework supports comparison without collapsing revelation, text, manuscript, tradition, and interpretation into one category.
