# Divine Text Verification: Research Paper v3

## Abstract
This paper proposes a source-critical framework for studying Abrahamic scripture without collapsing revelation claims, surviving texts, manuscripts, traditions, and interpretations into one category.

## Research questions
1. What does each tradition explicitly identify as revelation?
2. What texts survive today?
3. How close are surviving witnesses to the relevant historical period?
4. What textual variants are documented?
5. Can a surviving text be identified with a named revelation claim, or does the evidence stop at plausibility?

## Method
Each claim receives a source type, source level, provenance, textual evidence, historical evidence, corroboration state, interpretation gap, and—only when data are sufficient—an evidence score.

## Important safeguard
A high textual-preservation score does not establish divine origin. Conversely, absence of an autograph does not by itself establish falsity.

## Case studies
- Muhammad / Quran / Hadith
- Musa / Taurat / Torah-Pentateuch
- Dawud / Zabur / Psalms
- Isa / Injil / canonical Gospels

## Canon model
Bible canons are represented by tradition-specific profiles rather than a single universal canon.

## Limitations
The full hadith corpus and primary Torah/Psalms/Gospel corpora are not yet locally ingested in this runtime. Those states remain explicit in the project registry.

## Conclusion
The engine is a reproducible research baseline, not an automated religious adjudicator. Final claims require source review and human scholarly review.
