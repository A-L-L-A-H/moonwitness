# Quran Audit Module — Phase 2A

## Scope

This module audits the Quran as a research object without collapsing three distinct questions:

1. **Revelation claim** — what the Quran claims about its origin.
2. **Textual transmission** — what manuscripts, readings, and written witnesses show.
3. **Metaphysical origin** — whether God is the ultimate cause of the revelation. This is not assigned a numerical manuscript score.

## Evidence layers

- **L6** — revelation is explicit in the source's own claim.
- **L3** — transmitted text / manuscript evidence.
- **L7** — reserved for a textual-strength conclusion only after a comparative audit.

## Required fields

- claim
- Quran references
- manuscript witnesses
- dating method
- dating limitations
- variant category
- transmission summary
- historical assessment
- **CRAYON layers (v7.0.0)** (R, G, B, L, Caution)
- **project score (bottom-up)** (Source, Transmission, Proximity, Corroboration, Gap)

## Current baseline evidence

The University of Birmingham reports that its Quran manuscript (Mingana Islamic Arabic 1572a) contains parts of Surahs 18, 19 and 20. Its parchment has a radiocarbon range of **568–645 CE at 95.4% probability**; the university also notes that radiocarbon dates the parchment material, while paleography identifies the script as Hijazi and places the manuscript in the seventh century.

## Anti-overclaim rules

- Early manuscript evidence does **not** prove divine origin.
- A parchment date does **not** equal an exact ink-writing date.
- Documented reading/textual variation does **not** by itself prove a separate competing scripture.
- Absence of a known variant does **not** prove zero historical variation.

## Comparative rule

The same evidence fields must later be applied to Torah/Pentateuch, Psalms, and the four canonical Gospels.
