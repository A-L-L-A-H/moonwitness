# Research Methodology

## Research question

The engine asks whether textual, lexical, narrative, contextual, and empirical trait relations can be represented in an auditable graph without collapsing distinct epistemic layers.

## Method

1. Define a claim narrowly.
2. Search Qur'an first under the project source policy.
3. Add Taurat/Zabur/Injil witnesses only when useful and source-label them.
4. Separate explicit wording from lexical/root interpretation and model inference.
5. Preserve uncertainty and alternative hypotheses.
6. Derive graph edges only after provenance exists.
7. Compute scores from frozen/versioned formulas.
8. Validate invariants and run automated tests.
9. Present scores as indices, not metaphysical probabilities.

## Falsifiability and correction

A claim can be downgraded or removed when:

- the cited passage does not contain the claimed wording;
- a lexical root was misidentified;
- identity binding is weaker than represented;
- a conflict was incorrectly generalized;
- an automatic matcher caused double counting;
- a historical-language or geography assumption was overstated.

Corrections must create a new dataset/version rather than silently changing the interpretation of an old report.

## External science layer

Psychology/value taxonomies and surveys are external evidence layers. They may evaluate real-world trait salience or human preference but cannot alter the textual evidence class of a scripture claim.
