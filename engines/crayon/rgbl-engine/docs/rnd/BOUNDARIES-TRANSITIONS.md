# RGBL Boundaries and Transition Protocol

## Boundary test

When classifying a statement, ask for its **primary epistemic function**, not merely the words it contains.

| Level | Admissibility question | Typical claim types |
|---|---|---|
| R1 | Is this primarily an observation, measurement, source record, or context statement? | observation, measurement, source-statement |
| G2 | Is this primarily a supported pattern, relation, model, rule, mechanism, or causal hypothesis? | pattern, relation, model, inference |
| B3 | Is this primarily about being, agency, intention, relationship, impact, value, responsibility, or meaning? | entity, agency, intention, impact, interpretation, value |
| L4 | Is this primarily an integrated lesson, caution, option, decision, action, or responsible direction? | decision, caution, value, theological recognition when functioning as integrated conclusion |

Some types are context-dependent. `value` may be B3 when describing what matters to a being and L4 when expressing a value to preserve in a decision. A `theological-claim` may function at B3 or L4, but its provenance must remain explicit.

## Transition R1 → G2

Rule: a G2 claim identifies R1 information or source material that supports it.

Supported when:
- relevant R1 support exists; and
- provenance/dependency is recorded where structured claims are used.

Partial when support exists but the bridge is weak, sparse, or not fully traced.

Unsupported when no R1 support is recorded.

A single observation can motivate a hypothesis, but repeated-pattern language from one event should be marked provisional (`G_PATTERN_JUMP`).

## Transition G2 → B3

Rule: B3 explains how a supported pattern bears on a being/entity, agency, relation, impact, value, or meaning.

Risks:
- projecting intention from behavior alone;
- turning one act into a permanent identity;
- skipping the mechanism/pattern and jumping directly from an event to a meaning claim.

## Transition B3 → L4

Rule: L4 integrates prior levels into a proportionate direction while exposing limitations.

A decision is weaker when:
- B3 impact/meaning has not been examined;
- alternatives remain unresolved;
- confidence is low;
- contradictory evidence remains open.

Low-confidence actions should prefer reversibility, information gathering, or limited downside where appropriate.

## Transition L4 → R1'

A decision is not evidence that its expected benefit occurred.

```text
L4 action
→ execute
→ observe consequence
→ new R1
→ rerun RGBL
```

The engine therefore marks the feedback transition `pending` until new Reality is recorded.

## Prohibited jumps

The following are quality failures unless explicitly justified:

```text
G without R   → pattern/model without information support
B without G   → meaning/agency claim without an explicit interpretive bridge
L without B   → recommendation before beings/impact/meaning are considered
L despite unresolved conflict → conclusion suppresses contradictory evidence
```

These warnings do not prove the claim false. They identify that the recorded reasoning chain is incomplete.
