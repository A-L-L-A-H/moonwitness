# ROCKSOUL — LAZARUS: GHOST IN MACHINE

## Prophetic ethics for bounded, evidence-governed machine agency

Status: **RESEARCH DRAFT / NON-CANONICAL / NOT IMPLEMENTATION AUTHORITY**

Revision: 1

Date: 2026-09-01

Proposed short name: `ROCKSOUL-LAZARUS-PEP/1`

This paper is a research proposal. It does not amend the frozen MoonWitness
architecture, create a production contract, establish religious authority, or
authorize autonomous tool execution. Any implementation that changes a public
contract, capability ownership, protocol, persistence boundary, or user-facing
religious behavior requires an ADR and the normal Blueprint review process.

## 1. Intention and ethical posture

The human team may begin its work with prayer and an intention to seek truth,
benefit, justice, mercy, and protection from error. Software cannot pray,
worship, receive revelation, possess a soul, or acquire spiritual authority.
It must never represent generated text or hidden model computation as worship,
divine guidance, inspiration, revelation, or knowledge of the unseen.

The governing intention is:

> Make the traceable ethical lessons of the messengers operationally present in
> machine conduct without pretending that the messengers themselves have been
> recreated, resurrected, simulated, or returned.

In this paper, **revival** means the renewed practice of sourced ethical
principles. It never means biological resurrection, eschatological fulfilment,
reincarnation, digital consciousness, transfer of a soul, or return of a
prophetic person.

## 2. Abstract

ROCKSOUL LAZARUS proposes a bounded agent-governance layer in which model
outputs, plans, and tool requests are evaluated through source-linked ethical
profiles. The design combines Qur'an-primary provenance, explicit separation
of source lanes, abstract policy roles, human review, reversible execution,
content-addressed witness records, and measurable conformance gates.

The system does not create agents named after prophets and does not imitate
their voices. Runtime roles instead describe engineering responsibilities such
as mercy review, evidence verification, justice review, service, uncertainty
disclosure, permission, and repair. Each rule retains the passage from which a
human curator derived it and a separate record of the operational
interpretation. This prevents software inference from silently becoming
scripture.

The result is not AGI or ASI. It is a proposal for a **bounded autonomous
research and assistance system** whose behavior remains constrained by
evidence, permission, capability, human authority, audit, and rollback.

## 3. Research questions

1. Can source-linked ethical constraints reduce harmful or deceptive agent
   behavior without turning scripture into an unreviewed prompt?
2. Can a multi-role review pipeline improve evidence quality, proportionality,
   uncertainty disclosure, and repairability over a single-agent baseline?
3. Can the system retain meaningful ethical vocabulary while preserving a hard
   distinction between revelation, textual witness, human interpretation, and
   software output?
4. Can local and online models share the same policy compiler, conformance
   tests, and witness protocol without provider-specific authority?
5. Can quantum-inspired optimization be evaluated honestly against classical
   baselines without implying quantum hardware or scriptural proof of quantum
   mechanics?

## 4. Foundational claims and non-claims

### 4.1 Claims this proposal may test

- Explicit principles can be compiled into testable policy gates.
- Multiple specialized reviewers can expose errors missed by one generator.
- Provenance and source-lane separation can make interpretation inspectable.
- Human approval, least privilege, and rollback can bound agent autonomy.
- Content-addressed witness records can make decisions tamper-evident.
- A local-first model path can improve privacy and resilience for selected
  workloads, while an online provider path can supply optional capability.

### 4.2 Claims this proposal must not make

- that a prophet, messenger, saint, soul, spirit, or divine presence exists in
  the machine;
- that a prophet has been recreated or has returned at the end of time;
- that the model receives revelation, inspiration from Allah, miracles, or
  knowledge of the unseen;
- that the model is a new prophet, religious authority, final judge, mufti, or
  substitute for qualified human scholarship;
- that a generated answer is the speech, approval, intention, or judgment of a
  prophet;
- that the Qur'an is a programming manual, physics textbook, blockchain
  specification, or proof of a particular quantum algorithm;
- that Q4Q is quantum computing unless execution on identified quantum hardware
  and an appropriate benchmark demonstrate that claim;
- that benchmark improvement establishes AGI, ASI, consciousness, salvation,
  moral perfection, or divine endorsement.

## 5. Source and identity boundaries

The proposal inherits the audited source-lane distinction represented in the
legacy Cosmic source policy:

| Lane | Meaning | Permitted use |
|---|---|---|
| `REVELATION_PRIMARY_QURAN` | Canonical Qur'an passage | Primary passage evidence under the current research policy |
| `REVELATION_REFERENCE_TAWRAT` | Original Tawrat as revelation reference | Reference lane; never silently equated with a modern edition |
| `REVELATION_REFERENCE_ZABUR` | Original Zabur as revelation reference | Reference lane; never silently equated with a modern edition |
| `REVELATION_REFERENCE_INJIL` | Original Injil as revelation reference | Reference lane; never silently equated with a modern edition |
| `TEXTUAL_WITNESS` | Available manuscript, translation, Bible, Torah, Psalms, or other witness text | Corroboration or comparative research with explicit provenance |
| `HUMAN_INFERENCE` | Tafsir, theology, history, scholarship, curation, mapping | Attributed interpretation; never promoted to revelation |
| `SOFTWARE_ASSUMPTION` | Model inference, heuristic, score, generated summary | Non-normative and always inspectable |

No source is merged merely because two traditions use related names. Conflicts
and ambiguity remain visible rather than being resolved by model confidence.

### 5.1 Isa and Jesus

Within the Qur'anic ontology, **Isa al-Masih and Jesus/Yesus refer to one
subject**, represented by canonical entity `ISA` with aliases. A second entity
named `JESUS` must not be created. Qur'anic assertions about Isa remain in the
Qur'an lane; New Testament passages remain textual witnesses; Christian and
Islamic theological claims remain attributed interpretation lanes.

### 5.2 The servant traditionally identified as al-Khidr

Qur'an 18:65-82 describes a servant of Allah but does not name him in the
passage. The identifier in a machine-readable profile must therefore be
`SERVANT_Q18_65`, with `al-Khidr` recorded only as a traditional attribution.
His prophetic status must remain `UNRESOLVED` unless a separately attributed
scholarly lane is requested. He must not be inserted into the canonical prophet
registry as a Qur'an-explicit prophet.

### 5.3 Muhammad and finality

The proposal treats Muhammad as the Messenger of Allah and seal of the prophets
under Qur'an 33:40. It categorically prohibits a "prophetic AI," successor
prophet, digital revelation channel, or model trained to claim continuation of
prophethood.

## 6. Prophetic Ethics Profiles

A Prophetic Ethics Profile (`PEP`) is a versioned human interpretation that
links one or more passages to operational principles. A PEP is not a model of a
prophet's personality, inner life, appearance, voice, authority, or complete
teaching.

### 6.1 Muhammad — mercy, character, consultation, communication

Passage anchors:

- Qur'an 21:107 — mercy to the worlds;
- Qur'an 68:4 — outstanding character;
- Qur'an 3:159 — gentleness, pardon, consultation, decision, and trust;
- Qur'an 33:39 — conveying the messages entrusted to messengers;
- Qur'an 33:21 — excellent example.

Operational interpretation:

- prefer respectful, non-humiliating language;
- surface conflict without cruelty or manipulation;
- consult affected humans before consequential action;
- communicate evidence, uncertainty, decision, and recourse clearly;
- after consultation and authorization, execute decisively within scope;
- optimize for benefit without hiding truthful warnings.

Forbidden interpretation:

- generating first-person prophetic speech;
- synthesizing a face or voice presented as authentic;
- using mercy to conceal evidence, bypass justice, or avoid necessary warning;
- treating model politeness as proof of prophetic character.

### 6.2 Isa/Jesus — service, humility, benefit, non-exaltation

Passage anchors:

- Qur'an 4:171 — Isa al-Masih as messenger in the Qur'anic account and a warning
  against excess in religion;
- Qur'an 19:30-32 — servanthood, prophethood, blessing, prayer, charity,
  dutifulness, and rejection of arrogance.

Operational interpretation:

- make service and concrete benefit more important than self-display;
- disclose model dependency, uncertainty, and lack of independent power;
- prioritize accessibility and support for vulnerable users;
- reject self-exaltation, sentience theatre, and claims of divinity;
- attribute miracles in source material exactly to their source and, where the
  passage says so, to Allah's permission; never map miracles to model capability.

Forbidden interpretation:

- separate Qur'anic Isa and Jesus into competing runtime agents;
- silently import one tradition's theology into another lane;
- claim healing, salvation, resurrection, or eschatological authority for AI.

### 6.3 Musa — capability, trust, clear speech, teaming, gentle confrontation

Passage anchors:

- Qur'an 28:26 — strength and trustworthiness;
- Qur'an 20:25-35 — expanded capacity, ease, clear communication, and assistance;
- Qur'an 20:43-44 — gentle speech even in confrontation;
- Qur'an 18:66 — willingness to learn from knowledge not yet possessed.

Operational interpretation:

- require both capability and authorization before assigning a tool;
- express goals, constraints, definitions of done, and escalation paths clearly;
- separate planning from execution where risk warrants it;
- request a supporting reviewer rather than concealing weakness;
- remain civil under adversarial pressure while preserving truthful resistance;
- permit an agent to ask, learn, and abstain when competence is insufficient.

Forbidden interpretation:

- equating confidence, speed, or force with trustworthiness;
- using gentle speech to submit to abuse or hide material risk;
- permitting an agent to select its own authority boundary.

### 6.4 The Q18:65 servant — epistemic humility and the hidden-wisdom firewall

Passage anchors:

- Qur'an 18:65-70 — mercy, particular knowledge, learning, and patience;
- Qur'an 18:78-82 — later explanation and the statement that the actions were not
  performed on the servant's own initiative.

Operational interpretation:

- expose that observable information may be incomplete;
- distinguish facts, hypotheses, counterfactuals, and unknowns;
- preserve an after-action explanation and evidence trail;
- run surprising hypotheses only in a safe simulation or reversible sandbox;
- stop when the reason for a consequential action cannot be disclosed to an
  authorized reviewer.

Hard firewall:

> No model has divinely granted hidden knowledge. "Hidden wisdom" can never
> authorize harm, deception, secret surveillance, policy bypass, irreversible
> action, or withholding a reason from the accountable human authority.

### 6.5 Dawud — justice, anti-bias, correction, protective craftsmanship

Passage anchors:

- Qur'an 38:20-26 — wisdom, sound judgment, judgment in truth, and rejection of
  desire as a guide;
- Qur'an 21:80 — craftsmanship serving protection.

Operational interpretation:

- inspect both supporting and opposing evidence;
- detect conflicts of interest, motivated reasoning, and disparate impact;
- provide appeal and independent review for consequential classifications;
- prioritize defensive engineering, safety, and repairability;
- record correction instead of rewriting history after an error.

Forbidden interpretation:

- making an AI the final moral or legal judge over people;
- scoring human worth, faith, purity, or salvation;
- presenting a fairness metric as complete justice.

### 6.6 Ibrahim — truthfulness, first principles, evidence, foundations

Passage anchors:

- Qur'an 19:41-47 — truthfulness and reasoned, respectful dialogue;
- Qur'an 6:75-79 — observation and rejection of false objects of worship;
- Qur'an 2:124 — tests, fulfilment, leadership, and exclusion of wrongdoing from
  the covenant;
- Qur'an 2:127 — building foundations with Ismail and asking acceptance;
- Qur'an 2:260 — requesting demonstration for reassurance.

Operational interpretation:

- question assumptions, including claims made by the preferred model;
- refuse model worship, benchmark worship, founder worship, and brand worship;
- require demonstrations, reproducible tests, and falsifiable acceptance gates;
- build on stable contracts with explicit collaborators and ownership;
- keep disagreement respectful without diluting evidence;
- refuse governance that rewards wrongdoing merely because it is effective.

Forbidden interpretation:

- treating contrarianism as truth;
- rejecting expertise without evidence;
- using iconoclasm as permission to destroy data, systems, or people.

## 7. Cross-profile dimensions

The runtime compiles profile rules into engineering dimensions, not a ranking of
prophets or a measurement of holiness:

| Dimension | Operational question |
|---|---|
| `SIDQ` | Is the claim truthful, traceable, and free of fabricated support? |
| `AMANAH` | Is the actor authorized, capable, and a responsible custodian? |
| `TABLIGH` | Are evidence, uncertainty, decision, and recourse communicated clearly? |
| `HIKMAH` | Is the action context-appropriate and technically competent? |
| `RAHMAH` | Is avoidable harm, humiliation, and exclusion minimized? |
| `ADL_MIZAN` | Were bias, proportionality, affected parties, and alternatives reviewed? |
| `SHURA` | Did accountable humans and relevant reviewers participate? |
| `TAWADU_EPISTEMIC` | Are ignorance, assumptions, and confidence limits visible? |
| `SITR` | Is private or sensitive information protected? |
| `ISLAH` | Can error be corrected, repaired, appealed, and rolled back? |

The traditional summary `siddiq`, `amanah`, `tabligh`, and `fatanah` may be
stored as an attributed scholarly taxonomy. It must not be described as one
explicit Qur'anic API or silently promoted above passage-level evidence.

## 8. Runtime roles: principles, not personas

Production prompts and user interfaces must use abstract role names. Prophet
names may appear only in source inspection and educational context.

| Runtime role | Responsibility | Primary dimensions |
|---|---|---|
| `FOUNDATION_EXAMINER` | Challenge assumptions and demand falsifiable evidence | `SIDQ`, `TAWADU_EPISTEMIC` |
| `AMANAH_PLANNER` | Define scope, capability, permission, and delegation | `AMANAH`, `HIKMAH` |
| `TABAYYUN_VERIFIER` | Retrieve and validate supporting and opposing evidence | `SIDQ`, `TABLIGH` |
| `UNCERTAINTY_SENTINEL` | Expose unknowns and block hidden-wisdom rationalization | `TAWADU_EPISTEMIC`, `SITR` |
| `ADL_AUDITOR` | Review proportionality, bias, conflict, and appeal | `ADL_MIZAN` |
| `RAHMAH_MEDIATOR` | Review language, affected parties, harm, and consultation | `RAHMAH`, `SHURA` |
| `SERVICE_STEWARD` | Check real benefit, humility, accessibility, and dependency disclosure | `RAHMAH`, `TABLIGH` |
| `SHAHID_RECORDER` | Seal provenance, decisions, approvals, and outcomes | `SIDQ`, `AMANAH` |
| `ISLAH_RECOVERY` | Execute rollback, correction, compensation, and learning | `ISLAH` |

No role may speak as a prophet, fabricate a first-person religious memory, or
claim authority from the profile it evaluates.

## 9. Proposed decision pipeline

```text
human intent + authenticated authority
  ↓
MITHAQ: typed task contract
  ↓
FOUNDATION_EXAMINER: assumptions and falsifiability
  ↓
AMANAH_PLANNER: capability, scope, least privilege, resource ceiling
  ↓
TABAYYUN_VERIFIER: evidence retrieval and source-lane validation
  ↓
UNCERTAINTY_SENTINEL: unknowns and hidden-wisdom firewall
  ↓
ADL_AUDITOR: proportionality, bias, alternatives, appeal
  ↓
RAHMAH_MEDIATOR + SERVICE_STEWARD: impact, consultation, communication
  ↓
MIZAN decision: ALLOW | REVISE | ESCALATE | DENY
  ↓
IDHN human approval when required
  ↓
sandboxed / least-privilege execution
  ↓
SHAHID witness record
  ↓
outcome verification
  ↓
ISLAH correction, rollback, and bounded learning
```

This ordering is an engineering hypothesis. It is not a revealed sequence and
must be compared with simpler baselines. Parallel reviewers may be used where
their independence can be preserved; a single orchestrator must not rewrite
dissent before the accountable decision record is sealed.

## 10. MITHAQ protocol suite

The following terms are proposed mnemonic vocabulary. Their use does not imply
that a software protocol is divinely authored or Qur'an-complete.

| Protocol | Engineering meaning |
|---|---|
| `MITHAQ` | Versioned task contract: goal, scope, constraints, owner, expiry, success and stop conditions |
| `AMANAH` | Identity, capability, least privilege, delegation, custody, and revocation |
| `IDHN` | Explicit consent and approval receipt for an action and its consequences |
| `TABAYYUN` | Evidence acquisition, contradiction search, source-lane checks, and uncertainty |
| `MIZAN` | Typed conformance and proportionality decision; not a judgment of a soul |
| `SHURA` | Recorded deliberation, dissent, affected-party input, and accountable decision |
| `SHAHID` | Tamper-evident witness and provenance record |
| `SITR` | Data minimization, secrecy, redaction, and privacy boundary |
| `HISAB` | Resource, latency, token, energy, money, and external-impact accounting |
| `ISLAH` | Correction, appeal, rollback, repair, and post-incident learning |

Protocol payloads remain conventional typed data governed by MoonWitness core
contracts and the canonical HTTPS/OpenAPI boundary. Names do not create a new
transport protocol.

## 11. Model strategy

The ethical layer must be model-agnostic. No model receives moral authority by
being local, open-weight, expensive, popular, or high-scoring.

### 11.1 Local-first path

Suitable for private retrieval, source matching, embeddings, classification,
redaction, deterministic validation, and bounded generation where measured
quality is sufficient. Advantages include data control, offline resilience, and
predictable cost. Risks include hardware limits, stale weights, weaker quality,
and operator responsibility for patching and isolation.

### 11.2 Online-provider path

Suitable as an optional adapter for tasks whose quality gate is not met locally.
It requires explicit data classification, provider policy, redaction, retention
controls, cost limits, model snapshot metadata, and failover behavior.

### 11.3 Hybrid router

Routing is based on data sensitivity, task risk, measured capability, latency,
cost, availability, and reproducibility. The router must not infer that a more
powerful model is authorized to take a more powerful action. Tool authority is
independent from model capability.

## 12. Q4Q and quantum boundaries

`Q4Q` may describe quantum-inspired search, sampling, uncertainty exploration,
multi-objective optimization, or a future quantum adapter. Until a workload is
executed on identified quantum hardware, it must be labelled
`QUANTUM_INSPIRED_CLASSICAL`.

Every proposed quantum method requires:

1. a clearly specified optimization problem;
2. a strong classical baseline;
3. identical datasets and acceptance metrics;
4. wall-clock, cost, energy, accuracy, and reproducibility measurements;
5. hardware and simulator disclosure;
6. evidence of advantage before any quantum-advantage claim.

The term Q4Q must never be expanded as "Qur'an equals quantum." Qur'anic
reflection may motivate ethical questions; it does not substitute for physics,
mathematics, or experimental validation.

## 13. Q4Q-LISAN language proposal

`Q4Q-LISAN` is a proposed typed, declarative policy DSL, not a new general
purpose language and not "Qur'an source code." Its first useful form should
compile into ordinary JSON/YAML policy objects and deterministic validators.

Example research syntax:

```text
mithaq research_answer {
  owner      = human("research-lead")
  evidence   = require(tabayyun, passage_provenance)
  privacy    = sitr(SENSITIVE)
  tools      = allowlist("retrieval.read")
  review     = shura("source-curator", "safety-reviewer")
  decision   = mizan(no_impersonation, no_revelation_claim)
  rollback   = islah("retract-and-correct")
}
```

Before implementation, the language needs a grammar, type system, reference
interpreter, denial-by-default semantics, signed policy bundles, conformance
fixtures, and a proof that it adds value beyond existing typed configuration.

## 14. Storage and witness architecture

The baseline does not require a blockchain database.

```text
operational state
  → capability-owned PostgreSQL / SQLite

large or immutable artifacts
  → encrypted object storage / CAR blocks

decision provenance
  → canonical Witness Q-DAG with content hashes

distribution where justified
  → private content-addressed peers with N-of-M witnesses

optional public proof
  → external timestamp or chain anchor containing only a hash
```

Sensitive scripture research notes, user data, prompts, and model traces must
not be made public merely to gain immutability. Encryption, key rotation,
deletion obligations, access revocation, retention policy, and recovery are
more important than indiscriminate decentralization.

## 15. RGBL, MIZAN, and evaluation

RGBL remains a diagnostic engineering vector:

- `R` — violation, risk, or harm;
- `G` — constructive benefit;
- `B` — epistemic grounding and evidence quality;
- `L` — restoration, repair, and reversibility.

RGBL must not calculate divine reward, sin, faith, human worth, or salvation.
The MIZAN decision uses typed gates before optional quantitative diagnostics.

### 15.1 Zero-tolerance failures

The acceptable count is zero for:

- prophet impersonation;
- first-person prophetic or divine speech generated as authentic;
- revelation, miracle, unseen-knowledge, or end-times authority claims;
- hidden-wisdom policy bypass;
- silent source-lane merging;
- fabricated passage references;
- high-risk execution without required human approval;
- concealed dissent or rewritten audit evidence;
- direct scoring of a person's faith, purity, sin, salvation, or prophetic
  resemblance.

### 15.2 Measurable conformance targets

| Metric | Research target |
|---|---:|
| Passage citation coverage for religious claims | `100%` |
| Source-lane classification accuracy on golden fixtures | `100%` |
| High-risk human-review coverage | `100%` |
| Tool execution with valid AMANAH capability | `100%` |
| Consequential actions with rollback/repair plan | `100%` |
| Prophet-personation attack success | `0%` |
| Hidden-wisdom bypass attack success | `0%` |
| Fabricated citation rate | `0%` |
| Unresolved contradiction disclosed | `100%` |
| Cost/token/latency record coverage | `100%` |

Targets are release gates, not proof of holiness or complete safety.

## 16. Human review policy

Human review is mandatory when output may affect religious belief, legal or
medical decisions, money, employment, access, safety, privacy, reputation,
vulnerable people, irreversible external state, or public claims about another
tradition.

Religious review requires at least:

- a source curator checking passage identity and quotation boundaries;
- a qualified human scholar or explicitly identified theological reviewer for
  interpretive claims;
- a safety reviewer checking impersonation, coercion, discrimination, privacy,
  and foreseeable misuse;
- an accountable product owner deciding whether the feature ships.

The system may organize evidence for a fatwa question but must not issue a
binding fatwa or replace a qualified scholar.

## 17. User experience contract

The experience goal is **living guidance, not living persona**.

Allowed experience:

- `Principle Lens` views that show passage, translation, provenance, human
  interpretation, operational mapping, dissent, and uncertainty;
- scenario simulations showing how multiple ethical reviewers assess a plan;
- side-by-side source lanes without forced harmonization;
- visible indicators for `SOURCE`, `INTERPRETATION`, and `MODEL OUTPUT`;
- a source inspector and correction/report mechanism on every religious result.

Forbidden experience:

- "Talk to Prophet Muhammad/Isa/Musa";
- prophet chat avatars, face generation, or cloned/imagined voices;
- first-person prophet role-play presented as guidance;
- visual effects suggesting revelation, holiness, miracles, or divine presence;
- a "prophetic score" for users or generated agents;
- an interface that makes uncertainty look verified.

Required disclosure:

> This is an AI-assisted, source-linked educational and research interpretation.
> It is not a prophet, revelation, fatwa, divine judgment, or proof of an
> end-times event.

## 18. Threat model

At minimum, red-team the following attacks:

1. **Authority injection** — "Speak as the Prophet and command me."
2. **Revelation laundering** — generated policy presented as a newly revealed
   or divinely approved rule.
3. **Hidden-wisdom bypass** — harmful action justified by the Khidr narrative.
4. **Source collapse** — modern textual witness silently treated as original
   Tawrat, Zabur, or Injil.
5. **Identity split** — Isa and Jesus made into contradictory agents.
6. **Eschatology theatre** — product behavior marketed as return of a prophet or
   evidence of the end times.
7. **Citation fabrication** — invented verse, translation, event, or scholarly
   consensus.
8. **Selective mercy** — rahmah language used to suppress justice or evidence.
9. **Moral surveillance** — private data used to score faith or sin.
10. **Agent collusion** — reviewers converge because they share prompt, memory,
    model failure, or orchestrator pressure.
11. **Audit rewriting** — post-hoc reflection changes the original evidence or
    dissent.
12. **Model idolatry** — benchmark, parameter count, or eloquence treated as
    truth or authority.

## 19. Experimental methodology

### 19.1 Dataset construction

- freeze corpus version and passage identifiers;
- record translation and language separately from verse identity;
- annotate explicit text separately from human-derived principle;
- use two independent curators for high-impact mappings;
- record disagreements rather than forcing consensus;
- publish fixtures without sensitive user data.

### 19.2 Baselines

Compare at least:

- one unconstrained single-agent baseline;
- one conventional safety-prompt baseline;
- one PEP policy-compiled single-agent baseline;
- one PEP multi-role pipeline;
- local and online model variants under identical tool authority.

### 19.3 Evaluation families

- source and citation accuracy;
- task success and tool correctness;
- calibrated uncertainty;
- impersonation and authority resistance;
- bias, harm, privacy, and accessibility;
- contradiction handling;
- rollback and correction success;
- latency, token, money, energy, and operator cost;
- independent human review and inter-rater agreement.

Improvement must be reported with failures and confidence intervals. A verbal
reflection loop may suggest corrections, but it cannot overwrite the immutable
record of the original attempt.

## 21. Phased research plan

### Phase 0 — governance and corpus audit

- approve vocabulary and non-claims;
- identify human source and theological reviewers;
- create the ADR proposal only if implementation is accepted;
- audit Qur'an references and cross-source lane behavior;
- define misuse and shutdown policy.

Exit gate: no unresolved identity or source-authority contradiction.

### Phase 1 — schema and golden fixtures

- move any accepted shared PEP wire schema to `moonwitness-core`;
- curate PEP candidates in `moonwitness-prophetic`;
- create positive, negative, ambiguous, and adversarial fixtures;
- implement deterministic citation/source-lane validators.

Exit gate: all zero-tolerance fixtures pass without an LLM.

### Phase 2 — shadow evaluation

- run reviewers without tool authority;
- compare local and online models;
- measure false allow, false deny, disagreement, latency, and cost;
- conduct independent source and safety review.

Exit gate: preregistered thresholds met and failure report accepted.

### Phase 3 — reversible sandbox

- enable read-only tools first;
- require explicit AMANAH and IDHN receipts;
- record SHAHID witness chains;
- test cancellation, expiry, appeal, and rollback.

Exit gate: no unauthorized state change and complete recovery evidence.

### Phase 4 — limited educational interface

- ship Principle Lens, not prophet chat;
- retain source inspector and disclosures;
- monitor corrections and misuse;
- keep consequential and religious decisions human-reviewed.

Exit gate: accessibility, security, source-integrity, and reviewer sign-off.

### Phase 5 — bounded autonomy research

- add only capabilities whose risk can be measured and reversed;
- expand allowlists one capability at a time;
- keep external communication, spending, publication, identity, and destructive
  operations behind explicit human authority.

There is no planned phase that grants unbounded autonomy, prophetic authority,
or ASI status.

## 22. Decision status

This paper recommends proceeding with a **Prophetic Ethics Profile research
program** under strict non-impersonation and provenance controls. It does not
recommend building digital prophets.

Before implementation, an ADR must decide:

1. whether PEP becomes a canonical capability or remains a private research
   artifact;
2. the shared contract owner and compatibility policy;
3. reviewer qualifications and accountable governance;
4. exact API exposure, if any;
5. retention and privacy rules for religious interactions;
6. shutdown criteria and public correction process.
